It has not been approved by NITA. It is written so that institutions can use it now to plan their work, and so that it can become official.

Few websites can meet the whole Service Standard at once. These levels let an institution start where it is, show progress, and know what comes next. Each level includes the one before it.

Level 1: Official and safe

People can tell the website is the government's, and using it is safe.

  • A .gov.gh address, served over HTTPS with HSTS (standard points 7 and 8).
  • The GGDS government website banner, header and footer on every page, with the footer's Accessibility, Privacy and Disclaimer links (point 5).
  • An accessibility statement and a privacy notice (points 3 and 9).
  • Supported software, with a date to upgrade each part before it stops getting security fixes (point 11).

How to show it: the GGDS check passes its address, banner and security-header checks.

Level 2: Usable by everyone

Every page works for everyone who needs it, on the phone they have.

  • Every page is built from GGDS components and patterns (point 5).
  • No failures in the GGDS check, on every page, in the release pipeline (points 3, 4 and 12).
  • Tested with a keyboard and a screen reader, and the accessibility statement says what was found (point 3).
  • Plain words on every page; nothing loaded from other websites, and no cookies the website does not need, before people agree (points 6 and 9).

How to show it: the GGDS check's report with no failures; the record of manual testing.

Level 3: A service people rely on

The service solves people's whole problem, in their language, and keeps getting better.

  • Designed and tested with the people who use it, including disabled people and people on basic phones (points 1, 2 and 3).
  • Published in the languages its users speak, translated and checked by speakers (point 6).
  • Its numbers are measured and published: how many people finish it, how long it takes, how satisfied they are (point 10).
  • The institution owns the code, content and accounts, and could change supplier without starting again (point 11).

How to show it: the evidence each point of the Service Standard asks for.

Using the levels

  • Say on the accessibility statement which level the website has reached, and when.
  • When you buy a website, ask for the level in the contract: see Buying a website or service.
  • Level 1 is the minimum for any government website. Aim for level 2 on every website and level 3 for every online service.
