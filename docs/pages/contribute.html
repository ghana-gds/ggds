GGDS belongs to every government institution that uses it. It is maintained by the National Information Technology Agency (NITA), its custodian (decision D-02), and anyone can contribute: ministries, departments and agencies, their suppliers, and members of the public.

Ways to help

  • Report a problem: something broken, unclear or inaccessible. Every page of this website has a link at the bottom to report a problem with it.
  • Share evidence: research findings, analytics, or what people said in testing about a component or pattern. Evidence is what most often changes GGDS.
  • Suggest a change to a page's guidance: every guidance page links to its source on GitHub.
  • Propose a component or pattern that your website needs and GGDS does not have (below).
  • Fix something in the code: see CONTRIBUTING.md for how to build and test GGDS, and how to sign off your commits.

Everything happens in the open, on GitHub. You need a free GitHub account. Everyone taking part follows the code of conduct.

Propose a new component or pattern

  1. Check it is not already there. Search this website and the open issues. Often an existing component, or a pattern built from several, does the job.
  2. Open a proposal with the "Propose a component or pattern" issue form. Say what people are trying to do, where you need it, and what you use today. Add screenshots and any research.
  3. Discussion. Other institutions say whether they need it too. NITA reviews proposals every month.
  4. Decision. NITA accepts the proposal, asks for more evidence, or explains why not. The decision and its reasons are written on the issue.
  5. Build and test. An accepted proposal is built as a beta component, with guidance, examples and tests, usually by the team that proposed it with NITA's help.
  6. Stable. It becomes stable once it is used on live government websites and has passed its accessibility checks.

What NITA looks for

A new component or pattern is accepted when:

  • It is needed: at least two institutions need it, or one service needs it and others are likely to.
  • It is different: no existing component does the job, even with small changes.
  • There is evidence it works for the people who use government websites in Ghana, or a plan to get that evidence in beta.

And before it is released, it must, like every GGDS part:

  • meet WCAG 2.2 level AA in automated tests, at desktop and phone widths;
  • work without JavaScript, and under a strict Content Security Policy;
  • fit a 320 pixel screen without sideways scrolling;
  • use GGDS tokens only, not new colours or sizes;
  • have guidance in plain English: when to use it, when not to, how, and what websites need to do for accessibility.

How changes are decided

  • Fixes and guidance changes are reviewed and merged by the GGDS maintainers at NITA.
  • Changes that affect every website, such as the default colours, the banner wording or what every website must have, are decided by NITA and recorded in the decision register, with the reasons.
  • Breaking changes follow the rules in Versions and support.

Report a security problem

Do not open a public issue. Follow the security policy to report it privately.
