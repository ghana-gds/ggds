Design pages and services from GGDS foundations, components and patterns, and test them with the people who will use them.

Start from what exists

  1. Patterns first: whole tasks such as asking for information or showing search results. Most services are one of them.
  2. Components next: the parts, each with when to use it and when not to.
  3. Foundations for anything new: colours with their measured contrast, the type scale and spacing. Use these values, not new ones.
  4. Example sites to see it all together: twelve government websites built with GGDS.

Design tools

There is no Figma or Sketch library yet. Until there is:

  • Design in the browser where you can. Every example has its HTML to copy, so a working prototype is often quicker than a drawing, and it shows real behaviour on a phone.
  • In a design tool, take colours, sizes and spacing from Foundations. Each value has a name (a token, such as --ggds-color-link): use the name in your notes, so developers use the same value.
  • Screenshot the live examples at 320px and 1280px wide to start a mock-up.

Your institution's look

  • Your institution changes one thing: its colour. Themes lists the ready-made ones and says how a new colour is checked, so text on it stays readable.
  • Use your approved logo. Until you have one, the header shows your initials.
  • Keep the rest the same as every other government website: the banner, the header layout, the gold focus ring, and the flag colours only as thin lines and bands. People trust what looks familiar.

Design for how people in Ghana use the web

  • Most visitors use a low-cost Android phone, often on a slow or expensive connection. Design at 320px wide first. Keep images few and small.
  • Many people share a phone or use one in bright sun: high contrast and large tap targets (at least 44px) matter.
  • Many people read English as a second language. Short sentences and familiar words help everyone.
  • Show every state, not only the happy path: errors, empty lists, no results, slow loading, nothing found.

Check your design

  • It works at 320px wide, and at 400% zoom without scrolling sideways.
  • Every interactive element shows the focus ring, and can be reached in a sensible order with the keyboard.
  • Meaning never depends on colour alone: errors have words, the current page is marked in more than one way.
  • Headings make sense on their own, in order.
  • You tested it with people who will use it, including disabled people and people on low-cost phones.

When GGDS does not have what you need

  • Check the patterns and the example sites first: the answer is often already there in another form.
  • If not, propose it: what people need to do, the evidence (research, analytics, calls), and your design. Proposals that several institutions need become components.
  • Until then, build it from GGDS tokens, so it fits and can be replaced later.
