Write government pages people understand the first time: plain English, clear titles and links, and the conventions every GGDS website shares.

Plain English

  • Write for someone in a hurry on a phone. Put the most important thing first.
  • Use short sentences (around 20 words) and everyday words: "buy" not "procure", "help" not "facilitate", "start" not "commence".
  • Write to the reader: "You can apply online", not "Applicants may submit".
  • Explain any term people need to know, such as "TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number)", the first time it appears.
  • Spell the British way (licence, organisation, colour), as Ghana does.

Pages

  • Give every page a title that says what it is for, such as "Apply for a courier licence". The browser tab shows it, and search engines use it.
  • Use one <h1>, the same as the title. Break the page up with headings that say what each part is about, in order (h2, then h3).
  • Write link text that makes sense on its own: "Download the application form (PDF, 2 pages)", not "Click here".
  • Put information in a web page, not a PDF, wherever you can. PDFs are hard to read on a phone and with a screen reader.
  • Give every image a short description in alt, or leave alt="" empty when it is only decoration.

Forms, buttons and errors

  • Ask questions the way people talk: "What is your mobile number?"
  • Label buttons with what will happen: "Send complaint", "Pay GH₵ 50".
  • Write error messages that say how to fix the problem: "Enter a date in the past", not "Invalid date".
  • Say what happens next after people send something, and how long it takes.

Other languages

  • If you publish a page in Twi, Ewe, Ga, Dagbani, Hausa or another language, mark it with lang, such as lang="tw", so screen readers pronounce it correctly.
  • Have translations checked by a fluent speaker who knows the subject, and keep them up to date with the English page.

Keep it current

  • Give every page an owner, and review it at least once a year.
  • Remove or update pages that are out of date. Old fees and deadlines cause more harm than a missing page.
