What adopting GGDS involves for an institution: what every website needs, what to ask suppliers for, and how to check it before launch.

Why use GGDS

  • People recognise an official government website and trust it, because every one looks and works the same way.
  • Accessibility, phone layouts and security settings are built in and tested, so each institution does not pay to solve them again.
  • It is free, open source (Apache-2.0) and needs no special tools: any web supplier can use it.

What every GGDS website has

  • The government website banner, header and footer.
  • Your institution's theme colour and approved logo.
  • Accessibility, Privacy and Disclaimer pages, linked from the footer. NITA's standards ask every government website for these.
  • An accessibility statement: how accessible the website is, what does not work yet, and how to report a problem.

The quickest way: make your website here

If your institution needs a website and has no developer, make your website: give your name, address, contact details and colour, choose what it needs, and download a complete GGDS website. It comes with the server settings for security and speed and a guide to putting it online on any hosting, including shared hosting with cPanel. Your team or supplier then rewrites the example words for your own services.

Keep the ggds-site.json in the download: it makes the same website again, with a newer GGDS.

Before you start

  1. Confirm your theme colour and logo. Ready-made themes still need each institution's approval.
  2. Name an owner for the website and its content, and someone who answers the feedback it gets.
  3. Find out what people come to do: your most-asked questions, calls and complaints. Those are your homepage's common tasks.

What to ask suppliers for

Put these in your terms of reference or contract:

  • Built with GGDS, at a named version, using its components and patterns without changing their look or behaviour.
  • Meets WCAG 2.2 level AA, shown by automated tests and by testing with disabled people before launch.
  • Works on a 320px-wide phone and on slow connections, and without JavaScript for reading and forms.
  • Runs under a strict Content Security Policy, with GGDS files served from your own website.
  • Your institution owns the code and content, and can move them to another supplier.
  • Updates GGDS when a new version is released, within an agreed time.

Check before launch

  • The website passes the GGDS check with no failures: npx @ggds/check https://your-address checks the pages, accessibility, security settings, email domain and speed, and says how to fix anything that fails.
  • Every page passes an automated accessibility check (such as axe), and key tasks were tested with disabled people.
  • Every form shows an error summary and clear messages when something is missing.
  • The website works on a low-cost Android phone on a mobile connection.
  • The banner names your real address, and the footer links work.
  • Someone is ready to answer the website's feedback and contact details.

Versions and support

  • GGDS is in draft (version ${version}). Version 1.0 comes after the external accessibility audit, and will be NITA guidance.
  • Every change is listed in What's new. Before 1.0, components marked "beta" may still change.
  • Report a problem or ask for something new on GitHub.
