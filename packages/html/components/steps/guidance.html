A few numbered things to do, in order, each with an icon, a short title and one sentence.

When to use

  • To say what to do in an emergency: “Move to higher ground”.
  • To explain how a service works before people start: “Check you can apply, apply online, pay, get your licence”.

When not to use

  • For more than 6 steps: use a numbered list in prose.
  • For what the institution will do next: use a timeline.
  • For things that can be done in any order: use a bulleted list or cards.

How to use

  • Use 3 to 6 steps. Start each title with a verb: “Switch off the electricity”.
  • Keep each to one short sentence under the title.
  • Choose icons that match the words; the words must work without them.
  • Add ggds-steps--urgent for an emergency, with an urgent notice or a page heading in red above.
  • Give the list a heading above it, such as “What to do now”, and make each title a heading one level below.

Do

  • Start each title with a verb.
  • Put the most urgent step first.

Don't

  • Use steps for decoration or marketing.

Accessibility

What GGDS does

  • It is an ordered list: screen readers say how many steps there are and which one they are on (WCAG 1.3.1).
  • The large numbers and the urgent colours have at least 4.5 to 1 contrast. The icons are hidden from screen readers.
  • Steps sit side by side on wide screens and one under another on phones, in the same order (WCAG 1.3.2).

What you need to do

  • Make each title a heading at the right level for the page.
  • Do not put links or buttons only in the icons.
