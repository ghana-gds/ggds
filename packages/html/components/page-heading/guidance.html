The top of an inner page in the institution’s colour: the page’s title, a line above it and a short summary.

When to use

  • At the top of an account, to welcome people and show what they have: “Welcome back, Ama”.
  • At the top of a warning page, with ggds-page-heading--danger: “Flood warning: low-lying areas near rivers”.
  • At the start of a service or a section that needs to stand out from ordinary pages.

When not to use

  • On the homepage: use the hero.
  • On question pages in a form: keep them plain, with the question as the <h1>.
  • On every page of a site: it loses its meaning.
  • For a site-wide message: use an urgent notice.

How to use

  • Put it first in <main>, full width, with a ggds-container inside.
  • Make the title the page’s <h1>.
  • Use the line above for context, such as the account holder or when a warning was updated. Keep the summary to one or two sentences.
  • Add key figures with ggds-key-figures--inverse if people need a few numbers first.
  • Use ggds-page-heading--danger only while a warning is in force.

Do

  • Make the title say what the page is for.

Don't

  • Put forms or long text in the page heading.

Accessibility

What GGDS does

  • It uses the theme’s deep colour, which every GGDS theme checks for 4.5 to 1 contrast with white text (WCAG 1.4.3). The danger colour is checked too.
  • Links in it are white and underlined.
  • In Windows contrast themes it keeps a border, and it prints as plain black text.

What you need to do

  • Make the title the page’s only <h1>.
  • If you set your own colour, check it with institutionCss, which refuses one too light for white text.
