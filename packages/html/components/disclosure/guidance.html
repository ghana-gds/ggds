Sections people can open and close, such as questions and answers.

When to use

  • For extra detail that only some people need, such as "What if I do not have a Ghana Card?".
  • For a list of questions and answers, where each person looks for one or two. Use an accordion.

When not to use

  • For information everyone needs, such as fees, deadlines or required documents. Put it on the page.
  • For short content. Showing it is quicker than opening it.
  • For steps people must follow in order. Use a numbered list.
  • To switch between views, like tabs. Use headings on one page, or separate pages with tab links.

How to use

  • Build each section from <details class="ggds-disclosure">, a <summary class="ggds-disclosure__summary"> and a ggds-disclosure__content block.
  • For several sections, put them in a ggds-accordion. It adds lines between them.
  • Write each summary as the question or topic people are looking for, in under ten words.
  • Start sections closed. Add open only when most people need that one.
  • Put only plain text in the summary: no links, buttons or headings.
  • Do not put a disclosure inside another one.

Do

  • Use the words people search for: "How do I pay?".

Don't

  • Hide the fee or the deadline in a closed section.
  • Write vague summaries such as "More information".

Accessibility

What GGDS does

  • Built on the browser's own <details>: it opens with Enter or Space, screen readers announce whether it is open, and it works without JavaScript.
  • The summary is at least 44px tall, bold and in the link colour, with the gold focus ring.
  • The arrow is drawn in CSS: it points down when closed and up when open.

What you need to do

  • Keep <details> and <summary>. Do not rebuild it with <div>s and a script.
  • Write each summary so it makes sense on its own.
  • Do not hide information everyone needs inside a closed section.
