Shows people how far through a task they are, such as “Question 3 of 4”, in words and as a bar.

When to use

  • On each question page of a service with 3 or more pages, so people know how much is left.
  • On a card for an application, to show which stage it has reached.

When not to use

  • On a service with 1 or 2 pages: it adds nothing.
  • When the number of pages depends on the answers and you cannot say it: say “Question 3” only, or leave it out. A total that changes is worse than none.
  • For an amount, such as rainfall: use a meter.
  • For time passing while something loads: GGDS pages should load fast enough not to need it.

How to use

  • Put it above the page’s <h1>.
  • Write the words in the label: “Question 3 of 4”, “Step 2 of 4: your canoe”, “Done”. The bar only repeats them.
  • Use a native <progress> with value and max: no style attributes, so it works under a strict Content Security Policy. Link the label to it with for and id.
  • Count the check answers page as a step, and leave out the confirmation page.

Do

  • Say the step in words.
  • Keep the total the same on every page.

Don't

  • Show percentages.
  • Make the bar a link or a button.

Accessibility

What GGDS does

  • The bar is a native <progress>, so screen readers say its name and how far along it is (WCAG 4.1.2).
  • The label is text with at least 4.5 to 1 contrast; the bar is not the only way to see the step (WCAG 1.4.1).
  • In Windows contrast themes the bar keeps an outline.

What you need to do

  • Give the <progress> a label, with for and id, so screen readers say what it measures.
  • Put the same words inside the <progress> element too, for browsers that do not draw it.
