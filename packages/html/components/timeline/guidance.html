What has happened and what happens next, in order: the stages of an application, a complaint or a case.

When to use

  • Under a confirmation panel, to say what happens next and when.
  • On a page where people track an application, to show where it has reached.
  • In a staff tool, to show a case’s history.

When not to use

  • For steps people must take themselves: use steps, or a numbered list.
  • For dates in a table of many records: use a table.
  • For more than about 7 stages: group them.

How to use

  • Write each stage as what happens, in a few words: “A fisheries officer checks your answers”.
  • Under it, say when: a date for what has happened, and a promise for what is to come (“Within 5 working days”).
  • Mark what is done with ggds-timeline__item--done and the stage it is at now with ggds-timeline__item--current. Leave the stages to come plain.
  • Start every stage with hidden words, “Done:”, “Now:” or “Next:”, so the state is said, not only drawn.

Do

  • Say when each stage happened or will happen.
  • Keep promises you can keep.

Don't

  • Show internal stages people cannot act on, such as “Sent to database”.

Accessibility

What GGDS does

  • It is an ordered list, so screen readers say the number of stages and each one’s place (WCAG 1.3.1).
  • Done and current stages differ in shape as well as colour (WCAG 1.4.1). In Windows contrast themes they stay different.

What you need to do

  • Add the hidden words “Done:”, “Now:” and “Next:” to each stage, as in the example.
  • Keep the stages the same for every applicant, so people can compare what they are told.
