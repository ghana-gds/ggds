One phone box that accepts a Ghana number however people write it, says how you will use it, and asks for the network only for mobile money.

When to use

  • When you will call people or send them an SMS about their application, complaint or appointment.
  • When you will pay people, or take payment, by mobile money.

When not to use

  • When you will never call or text. Do not ask for a phone number "just in case". Only collect the personal data you need.

How it works

  1. The question, such as "What is your phone number?", or a "Phone number" label in a page of contact details.
  2. The hint says how you will use it, and shows an example: "We will send you an SMS when we have made a decision. For example, 024 000 0000."
  3. One phone box, medium width, that accepts the number however people write it.
  4. For mobile money only, a separate question for the network, with radios.

Ghana phone numbers

  • A Ghana number has 10 digits and starts with 0, such as 024 000 0000 for a mobile or 030 000 0000 for a landline in Accra.
  • From abroad, or in international form, it is +233 and the same number without the first 0: +233 24 000 0000.
  • Do not reject a number because of its first 3 digits. Networks get new prefixes, and people keep their number when they move to another network.

The phone box

  • Use type="tel" and autocomplete="tel", with the ggds-input--width-medium class. Phones then show the number keypad and can fill in the person's own number.
  • Use one box. Do not split the number into several boxes, and do not make people choose a network or a country code from a list before they type.
  • Do not use type="number". It removes the first 0 in some browsers and does not allow + or spaces.
  • Say in the hint how you will use the number: SMS updates, a call from an officer, or both. If you will only send SMS, say "mobile number".

Checking the answer

  • Accept spaces, hyphens, dots and brackets, and remove them before you check: "024-000-0000", "(024) 000 0000" and "024 000 0000" are the same answer.
  • Accept all three Ghana forms: 0240000000, 233240000000 and +233240000000. Store one form, such as +233240000000, so you can send SMS to it.
  • Show the number back in groups, the way people say it: 024 000 0000.
  • Keep what people typed when there is an error, so they can correct it.

Numbers outside Ghana

  • People living abroad may not have a Ghana number. If your service can call or text abroad, accept any number that starts with + and a country code, such as +44 or +1, and say so in the hint.
  • If you can only use Ghana numbers, say so in the hint, and give another way to contact people, such as email.

Mobile money

  • Ask for the network only when you need it to pay or take a payment by mobile money. Ask it as a separate question with radios: MTN, Telecel and AT.
  • Do not guess the network from the number. People move their number between networks.
  • Ask "What is your mobile money number?", not "phone number", because some people use a different number for mobile money.
  • Show the number and network on the check answers page, so people can spot a mistake before money is sent.

Error messages

Use these messages in the error summary and next to the field.

  • If it is empty: "Enter your phone number".
  • If it has letters or other symbols: "Phone number must only include numbers, spaces, brackets, hyphens and +".
  • If a Ghana number has too few or too many digits: "Enter a phone number with 10 digits, such as 024 000 0000".
  • If a number from outside Ghana is not accepted: "Enter a Ghana phone number, such as 024 000 0000".
  • If the network is not chosen: "Select the network for your mobile money number".

Accessibility

  • Follow the accessibility advice in the ask for information pattern: a unique page title, the question as the heading, and a Back link.
  • Do not put the example in placeholder text. Put it in the hint, where it stays while people type.
  • Connect the hint and any error message to the box with aria-describedby, hint first, and add aria-invalid="true" on error.
  • Offer another way to be contacted, such as email or a letter, for people who cannot use a phone.
