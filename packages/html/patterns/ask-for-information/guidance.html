Break a form into short pages, let people check their answers, and tell them what happens next.

When to use

  • For any service that asks people for information: a complaint, an application, a registration, a request.

When not to use

  • For a search or a single question on a page, such as a newsletter sign-up. Use one field and a button.

How it works

  1. Start page. What the service is for, who can use it, what people need before they start (a Ghana Card, a receipt) and how long it takes. One "Start now" button.
  2. Question pages. One question, or a few closely related ones, per page. The question is the page's heading.
  3. Check your answers. Every answer in a summary list, each with a "Change" link, and the declaration, then the button that sends it.
  4. Confirmation. A confirmation panel that says it worked and gives the reference number, then what happens next and when, and how to get in touch.

Questions

  • Ask only what you need, and say why if it is not obvious, such as "We use your phone number only to call you about this complaint".
  • Ask in the words people use. "Which network are you complaining about?", not "Select operator".
  • Use radios for one choice from a short list, checkboxes for several, and a text input sized to the answer.
  • Add autocomplete to names, phone numbers, emails and addresses.
  • Do not ask for the same thing twice. Keep answers when people go back.

Errors

  • Check the answers when the page is sent, not while people type.
  • Show the error summary at the top, the message next to each field, and start the page title with "Error: ". With Django, {{ form }} and the GGDS form renderer do all three.
  • Say how to fix it: "Enter a mobile number with 10 digits, like 024 000 0000".
  • Keep what people entered, even when it is wrong, so they can correct it.

Confirmation

  • Give a reference number people can quote, and send it by SMS or email too.
  • Say what happens next and how long it takes, in working days.
  • Say how to contact you about it.

Accessibility

  • Give every page a unique title that starts with its question, such as "How should we contact you? – Make a complaint – NCA".
  • Do not time people out without warning. If you must, warn them first and let them ask for more time.
  • Let people go back without losing answers: a link at the top of each question page, and the browser's Back button.
  • Make a question the page heading by putting an <h1> inside the <legend> (with ggds-fieldset__legend--page), or the <label> inside an <h1> (with ggds-label--page), so screen readers hear it once.
