A homepage that leads with search and the most common tasks, then the main sections as cards.

When to use

  • On the homepage of every institution's website, and on the front page of a large section.

How it works

  1. The hero says in one line what the institution does, asks what people need, and offers search and up to six common tasks.
  2. Cards for the main sections, each with one sentence saying what is in it.
  3. News and notices come after the tasks, not before: most visitors come to do something.
  4. On phones, the tab bar keeps Home, three main sections and Search one tap away.

Choosing the tasks

  • Start from evidence: your website's most-visited pages and searches, and the questions your call centre and front desk get most.
  • Write tasks as things people do: "Make a complaint", "Check if a courier is licensed". Not department names.
  • Review them every few months, and when a deadline or a public notice changes what people need.

Search

  • Make the search work well before you put it first: it should find services by the words people use, including common misspellings.
  • It is a normal form (GET to your search page), so it works without JavaScript and results can be shared.

Accessibility

  • The hero's heading is the page's <h1>; card titles are <h2>.
  • Do not use a carousel or slideshow: people miss what moves past, and it is hard to use with a keyboard or screen reader.
