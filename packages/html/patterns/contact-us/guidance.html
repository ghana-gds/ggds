A contact page that first sends people to the task they came for, then gives every way to reach a person, when, and how long a reply takes.

When to use

  • For your website's contact page, linked from the footer of every page.
  • For the "Get help" part of a service, with the contact details for that service only.

How it works

  1. What people can do themselves. Before any phone number, link to the tasks people most often call about: check a status, make a complaint, find a form. Many people contact you because they could not find the page.
  2. Phone. The number, the hours it is answered, and what it costs if it is not free.
  3. Email, WhatsApp and social media, only the ones someone answers, each with how long a reply takes.
  4. Visit us. The office address in words people use, the landmarks near it, the GhanaPostGPS digital address and the opening hours.
  5. Complaints. A link to how to complain, so complaints do not get lost in general enquiries.

Writing contact details

  • Many people in Ghana would rather call than fill in a form. Put the phone number high on the page, not only in the footer.
  • Write phone numbers the way people dial them, in groups: 030 000 0000. Link them with tel: and the full international number, such as tel:+233300000000, so one tap calls on a phone.
  • Give a WhatsApp number only if it is answered. Say so if it is for messages only and not calls.
  • Say how long a reply takes, in working days, for every channel. "We reply within 2 working days" stops people writing again.
  • Give opening hours in a short table: days, times, and when you close for public holidays.
  • Do not ask people to use a contact form when an email address would do. If you use a form, follow the ask for information pattern and keep it short.
  • Check every number and address every few months. A wrong number is worse than none.

Saying where you are

  • Describe the location the way people give directions: the area, the street if it has a name, and a landmark they know, such as "opposite the district hospital".
  • Give the GhanaPostGPS digital address, such as GA-000-0000, so people and drivers can find the exact building.
  • Say which floor or office to go to, and what to bring, such as a Ghana Card.
  • A map is useful but must not be the only way to find you: maps use a lot of data, and screen reader users cannot read them. Give the address in words first, then a link to a map.
  • If you have regional or district offices, list them with the same details for each.

Accessibility

  • Make the phone number the link text, not "Call us here", and do not put it in an image.
  • Give each way to contact you a heading, so screen reader users can move straight to "Phone" or "Visit us".
  • Offer more than one way to get in touch: some people cannot call, and some cannot read or write easily.
